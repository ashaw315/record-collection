2px, derived colour — the journal's right edge, and nowhere else. It is the only rule on the page that is not grey and the only edge that is 2px. 5 · Colour

5.1 · Eight marks, one value, derived per record

One derived value fills every colour mark on the page, at one of the three steps of §5.5 and never at an opacity variant. The marks, in the order the eye takes them: the release-year field (the only one that carries type), the bar right of the sleeve, the flat triangle in the identity block's lower-left corner, the disc behind the rendered still, the quarter-circle in provenance, the quarter-circle in About, the journal's 2px edge, and the construction's coloured faces — eight drawn regions, one value. Two vocabularies, divided by the frame, and the rule is mechanical: a mark that touches a page edge is a flat plane of the derived colour; a mark that does not is an isometric solid. No judgement, nothing to infer, and it needs no appeal to what a mark is for — a build can check it.

An edge field's extent follows its height, not its cell: the corner triangle is 268 × 140, a width-to-height of 1.91. The width was unspecified and the build had picked 180, so the file needed the number — but the first attempt at this sentence derived one, giving 182 from the projection's box ratio, and that is a rule this file is not allowed to make: §0 says every number is read off the drawing, and the drawing has said 268 in all three instances since it was made. A derivation that lands 86px from the drawn mark is not a tighter statement of it, it is a second mark. The general form, which this project has now hit seven times: a number arrived at by reasoning is a proposal, and in a file whose premise is measurement it has to be checked against the drawing before it is written, not after. Tying extent to the cell remains the wrong term — the host cell is span 4 at 480px, and a 480px triangle is a band across the block rather than a corner mark — but the alternative to a cell-relative width is a measured one, not a derived one. Both other edge fields take their extent from the drawing the same way. The division is legible at the surface because the two kinds behave differently where it matters most, at the frame: a plane that continues past the view reports that the view is a window, and an object with a slice taken out of it reports that something went wrong. Nothing else on this page is cropped, so a cropped object has no company and reads as damage.

This retracts a ruling made one turn earlier, and the retraction is the more useful record. That ruling withdrew the flat marks entirely and extended the isometric vocabulary to the edges, on the argument that a flat edge mark is a division of labour no reader can see. Drawn side by side, the opposite held — which is the sixth time in this project that a decision taken on an argument was reversed by a drawing, and the second time in this section. The part that survives is the part that was tested by looking: a mark inside a cell is an isometric solid, because a small circle floating in a cell belongs to no system while a small solid belongs to this one. The rule now covers the tested case and the frame case separately, which is what it should have done first. And this one was caught by drawing it before the build rather than after — every earlier instance in this project was found by measuring something already assembled, which is the same correction arriving at the price of work already done. Two compositions differing in three marks cost less than one implemented ruling, and that is the transferable part: when a ruling extends a tested decision into an untested case, draw the extension rather than arguing it. The black block at the sleeve's edge is no longer an exception: it touches an edge and it is flat. Settled by looking rather than by argument — for marks inside a cell, which is the only case that drawing tested: the matrix solid reads as belonging to this page and a circle floating in a cell reads as a shape that could be on any page. The quarter-circles below are not that case; they touch an edge, and the frame rule sends them the other way. A circle is not part of any system here; a box at 30° is the page's own alphabet, and an ornament drawn in it is the construction appearing small rather than a decoration appearing near it. The construction is one mark however many of its faces carry colour (§5.5), and it is a separate entry from the disc behind it — one object visually, two entries here, because the faces are base step and the disc is tint. Two black marks sit against them, both fixed at ink and neither derived: the block at the sleeve's right edge and the square in About.

Only one of the eight carries type, and after §5.2's amendment it carries only the 72. The derivation clamps the value (§5.2) and the clamp is what guarantees the field its headline allowance; the separate constraint is that no 11px type sits on a base-step ground (§5.2 — tint is admitted as ground under type, with a contrast test on the type), so the release-year label sits on paper above the field and the field holds the 72 alone, ink, at 6.32:1 against a 3:1 allowance — and at no worse than 5.4:1 anywhere in the clamp band.

5.2 · The derivation — clamped base, and one rule about what may sit on it

Sample the cover's dominant chromatic value and clamp it: lightness into 0.62–0.74, chroma to at most 0.09, hue kept as sampled. The clamp was withdrawn for one round and is reinstated (§W.4). The withdrawal's reason was that the wall rendered the same value raw and a clamp on both screens compressed the axis the wall exists to display; the wall at rest is now monochrome, so no second screen reads the value and nothing outside this page constrains it. What the clamp is for, stated without reference to the wall: the field behind the 72 must clear its headline allowance on every record, and a band guarantees that in advance rather than testing it per record. At the null-spine record the unclamped value put the 72 at 1.00 against its own ground. A test on one cell rides with it: the release-year field has an 11px mono label on it, and 11px type needs 4.5:1 — the 72 is not the binding constraint, since headline-scale type has a 3:1 allowance the field never reaches for. Re-derived at the reinstated ground, and the numbers no longer force the rule: at the clamped 0.66 0.05 the field reads 6.32:1 against ink, and across the whole band ink never falls below 5.4:1. The figures that first produced the rule were measured at the sampled 0.55 0.038 — 4.05:1 ink and 2.93:1 paper, neither clearing 4.5 — and that ground is now drawn nowhere, so the claim that the per-record flip "has no answer on the record every raster shows" is withdrawn: at the clamped ground the answer is ink, on every record. The rule survives for a reason independent of the ratios, which is the honest version of it: the field is ground for one figure. An 11px mono label on it makes the field read as a labelled swatch and the 72 as its caption — the field stops being the thing the eye lands on, which §5.1 says is its whole job — and a per-record contrast flip would also reintroduce exactly the per-record test the clamp exists to remove. So it is stated at base step: no 11px type sits on a base-step ground. Base-step grounds carry headline-scale type only, which the 72 clears at 6.32:1 against its 3:1 allowance. Tint is admitted as ground under type of any size, with a contrast test on the type rather than a ban on its size — §9.4's full fill exists precisely to be ground under content, and a rule written "derived ground" captured it, which is the coarser-than-its-rationale shape one layer out from §9.2's. Over the fill at 0.791 0.016 the muted grey reads 2.6:1 and fails outright: its 11px date takes ink at 8.3:1 and its placeholder takes 0.34 at 5.1:1, which is the test doing the work the size ban was standing in for. The release-year label moves off the field onto paper — a 30px strip above a 120px coloured block, the band's 150px unchanged — and the field holds the 72 alone. That is the granularity finding once more and in the useful direction: the constraint belongs to the 11px label, so it is discharged by moving the label rather than by moving every record's colour.

Chroma is clamped down only. The floor is withdrawn, and the sampler is the reason. MIN_REGION_CHROMA at 0.04 is a rejection threshold: a cover with no qualifying region returns its linear-light mean precisely because there was no chromatic region to trust. Lifting to 0.045 at the sampled hue would therefore assert a hue the sampler explicitly declined to identify, on five of sixteen records — and an invented hue is worse than no hue, because nothing downstream can tell it from a real one. So: lightness clamped into the band, chroma clamped down only, no chroma floor — the floor is the one part of the clamp that stays withdrawn, and it is withdrawn for a reason about the sampler rather than about contrast. #bc4889 at 0.164 and #44946b at 0.101 keep their chroma, where the withdrawn ceiling brought them down. #9b9b9a at 0.001 and #363129 at 0.016 ship as near-greys at their own lightness — a warm or cool grey at the right value, which is what those covers are.

§5.5's ladder is what makes that survivable, and it is why this is the answer rather than the other one. Tint and shade derive from base, so a near-grey base gives near-grey steps: those five records render in grey and ink with a faint warm or cool cast, the construction still reads as an object because its three tones are lightness steps and not hue steps, and nothing looks broken — it looks like a record whose sleeve has no colour. 5.5 · Colour as a system, and where it stops

§28 defines this section’s “page” as the viewport, asserted at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844 only. At 1440 that is the 900 frame, which every figure here was measured against.

The page is keyed to one derived value, and being keyed is a set of relationships rather than a quantity of colour. The difference between a system and three placements is not how much there is — it is whether a reader can say what the colour means in each place without being told. Three rules do that; the fourth part is what protects them. This amends §5.1, which specified the marks and not their relationship.

One value, one ladder, three steps. The raw derivation of §5.2, then tint at 34% toward paper, base, and shade at 26% toward ink. No fourth step: three is the most a reader holds as distinct positions, and a fourth makes lightness look like it encodes something. Every coloured pixel on the screen is one of those three values.

The panel conflict, confirmed as the build resolved it, with the rule restated so it stops conflicting. Accent placement and tone derivation are two systems and the conflict came from reading one rule as governing both. Tone derivation is unchanged: top lightest, left base, right darkest, on every form. Placement is separate — an accent-bearing face takes the base step whichever face it is, so the panel, whose largest projected face is its right, carries base there and has no shade face. That does not breach the rule below; it means the rule below describes derivation and never placement. And the face is chosen by projected area, not by shape units — "top is the largest visible face" is true for beam, slab, cube and plate and false for the panel by seven times, which is what produced a 92.3/7.7 split in a path that was supposed to be traced between two ends.

The residual 87.7% worst case is not repaired, because the ratio is the wrong target — same finding as the budget, one level down. Two accent faces do not need to be equal; they need each to be findable. So the requirement on them is a floor apiece, not a ratio between them, and cube-with-plate at 2.10 against 7.84 passes if both clear it. The archetype set is left alone: changing it to equalise a ratio would be tuning the wrong axis, and the cube is small by design because the 6:1 band is what stopped the compositions reading as orderly.

The step says what kind of thing the mark is, and that is the system. Base is for marks that carry or frame the record's own material: the release-year field, the bar down the sleeve's edge, the journal's 2px edge, and the construction. The construction is one base mark however many faces carry colour — the coloured form's three plus the two accent tops are one object seen from three sides, not five marks, and every rule here that counts or sizes a mark counts it once and sizes it by its faces' combined area. The unit of counting belongs in this mapping rather than only in the enumeration below, which is where two later rules picked up the ambiguity. Tint is ground — the disc behind the construction, and the three flat edge fields — the corner triangle and the two quarter-circles. Shade is a face and never a shape: it exists only as the darkest side of a coloured form, which is the right-hand face on every archetype but a panel carrying an accent, where there is no shade face at all. A reader who notices that the strong marks always touch the record and the pale ones are always behind something has read it correctly, and there is nothing else to read.

Each band carries exactly two base marks. Identity band: the sleeve bar, and the construction — the same unit the mapping defines, counted once and including its accent tops. Record band: the year field, and the journal edge. This is the relationship §5.1 was missing — the same colour at the same strength in both halves of the page is what makes it one composition rather than a coloured lower half with accents above it.

Where it stops — five exclusions, and they are the restraint. No type is ever coloured, so no contrast floor on this page depends on the derivation. No grid rule is coloured but the single 2px journal edge — the structure is grey on every record. The paper is never tinted: one warm white on all seventeen, which is what lets the keyed marks read as keyed instead of as a theme. Nothing constant across records takes colour — nav, actions, labels, the two black blocks. No second hue and no gradient, because a derived hue plus anything is two claims about one record.

The area budget is withdrawn. It was authored, not measured, and it measures the wrong axis. This paragraph specified 12–18% of the page and asserted the budget was spent. Measured on the assembly it is 10.55% richest, 9.23% modal, 9.10% emptiest — under the floor on every record, re-measured this round because moving the 11px label off the year field cut the page's largest mark from 240 × 150 to 240 × 120 and took 1.56 points off every total (the pre-split figures were 12.11 / 10.79 / 10.66; a mark's area changing is exactly the event that makes an area figure stale, and this file has now had that class corrected in §5.2 and §4.2 as well) — which by its own wording puts almost every record in the state it names as the failure. But the figure being wrong is the smaller half. releaseYearField alone is 6.26% — 59% of all colour after the label split, 7.82% and two-thirds before it — everything else is under 1.5%. A page can hit any area target with one big rectangle, so an area percentage cannot tell a keyed composition from one large mark plus rounding, and 9.23% concentrated in a single cell is exactly the latter.

This is the general form again, and its most expensive instance: a constraint defined over an axis correlated with the signal rather than over the signal itself. The signal §5.5 is about is distribution — colour arriving at the same strength in both halves of the page — and total area is merely correlated with it. Same shape as the mean over a cover when the signal was hue, the fit over an arrangement when the signal was size, the shuffle over assignment when the signal was position. The tell is the same each time: the number moved and the thing it was meant to govern did not.

The 40% ceiling is withdrawn too — one turn after it was written, because it is the same defect a fifth time and I wrote it. A share-of-coloured-area ceiling is a ratio, and a ratio moves when either term moves: the emptiest record measures 68.8% against the richest's 59.3% not because any mark grew but because the edge fields suppress when their cells empty. Both figures are post-split — 73.4% and 63.6% before the 11px label moved off the field — and the pair moving together while the rule's subject did not is the clearest statement of why a ratio was the wrong instrument. The rule tightens hardest on the records with the least colour, which is the opposite of what it was for, and the arithmetic confirms no composition can satisfy it — reaching 40% needs total colour at 15.65% against a measured 10.55%, which would make the construction 56% solid colour and dominate the band the remedy was meant to protect. A ceiling no composition can reach is not a rule.

What replaces it is a floor on the small marks, not a ceiling on the large one, and that inverts the diagnosis. The year field at 59% is not the defect: it is the page's anchor, the one mark guaranteed on every record (§6), and the ground the 72 sits on. The defect is that the other marks are too small to be read as the same colour arriving again — the construction's coloured faces at 0.42% are the clearest case. So: every base-step mark must clear a floor, measured in the mark's own dimension. The year field is exempt, once and for this reason: it is the anchor, the one mark guaranteed on every record (§6), and a floor on it would be a floor on the thing that is never the problem. Tint marks are outside the floor entirely — the disc and the three edge fields are ground, and ground that is too small to find is ground doing its job. The count is already settled above at exactly two base marks per band; this adds a size and nothing else.

Two kinds of base mark, two measures. A field mark — in this layout, the construction — occupies area, and its floor is 0.5% of the page. A linear mark — the journal's 2px edge, the bar down the sleeve — is a line, and a line's findability is its length and its contrast, not its area: a 2px edge running a full cell height is instantly findable and will never reach 0.5% of anything. Their floor is extent: a linear mark runs the full length of the edge it marks, end to end, with no inset. Both currently pass it.

This is not the year field's exemption granted twice more, and the difference matters. The journal edge and the sleeve bar are not exempt from anything — they are measured on the axis that governs them, which is the sixth appearance of the same correction in this file: a rule defined over an axis correlated with the signal rather than over the signal itself. Area correlates with findability for a block and not for a line. The alternative readings both fail on inspection: widening the journal edge to ~20px makes a second rule weight at a thickness §3 does not have and destroys the "only 2px edge" that marks the one owner-written cell; widening the sleeve bar eats the cover, which §5.5 calls the source rather than a mark. A rule that can only be satisfied by breaking two other settled rules is measuring the wrong thing, not discovering two exceptions.

One consequence to carry into the build: the disc is tint and does not count toward the construction's base area. With it the construction measures 1.43% and clears; without it, 0.42% and fails. The failing number is the real one — a mark's floor is measured in its own step — and it reaches the remedy already named: grow the coloured faces, not the disc.

A floor is per-mark and absolute, so it does not move when the page empties, cannot be satisfied by one large rectangle, and cannot be gamed by suppression. Of the three ways out, this is the third — the year field does not shrink and the construction does not become a colour field; the construction's coloured faces, the one base field mark the floor governs, are where the page was actually failing. Total area may then land anywhere; nothing is keyed to it, and "more colour" is no longer a quantity anyone can argue about.

8 · The frame is the top of a longer page

The no-scroll constraint narrows to what it was always measuring: the record's facts fit one screen. Nothing is given up by saying so, because nothing in this file was ever spent on an interaction. The 900px budget in §2.1, the fixed bands, the one-diagonal rule, the mark floors, the title measure — every one was taken against a field that renders a value, and all of them survive the narrowing unchanged. A constraint scoped to what it measured is not a weakened constraint; it is a constraint that has stopped claiming territory it never surveyed.

The alternative is refused on this file's own evidence. Absorbing a file picker, a textarea, a live fetch and a price list into the frame means growing it or hiding four interactions behind modals — and the frame has 1px of slack at the year cell and 2px at the longest title (§4.2, §7), both already recorded as coincidences standing in for margins. Adding six controls to a frame that is exactly full would make that three, and the third would be the one that broke. It would also require specifying six interactions this file has never addressed, on a page whose every measurement was taken on facts — which is the extension-without-a-drawing failure this project has recorded seven times.

The fold is a composition edge, not a cut. The frame ends where §2.1 ends it — on the record band's closing rule, the tail having gone to the identity band — so the first screen closes on the same 1px edge every band closes on and the page below begins after a division rather than mid-band. No mark, rule or field may straddle 900, and the one element licensed to cross a rule (§7's construction) crosses a vertical inside the identity band and never the fold.

Below the fold takes this file's type, ink and grid, and not its band heights. Inherited: the scale with nothing between 40 and 15, mono uppercase labels at 11 with .10em tracking, 1px hairlines at oklch(0.72 0.004 80), the paper, the ink, the twelve-column full-bleed grid with its 1px cell verticals, and §5.5's ladder — every control label is a label, so the sections below are typographically this page. Not inherited: fixed band heights, and nothing else. Those exist because of a budget, and below the fold there is no budget — height is content-derived, sections stack, and a form that grows pushes the page down instead of overflowing a band. The grid stays because bands are height and columns are alignment (§9.1), so dropping the budget drops the height only. The derived colour crosses the fold at the base step and nowhere else: a base mark on four of the eight sections, selected by the rule in §9.4, plus ornament at the tint step (§9.2), and no construction — that stays the identity band's. The earlier version of this sentence barred ornament below the fold, which is what made the region read as correct rather than designed: a colour bar under a label is a compliance mark, and a page whose decoration stops at 900px is a composition with a document under it.

8.1 · No fact lives below the fold

The frame holds the facts that identify which record and which pressing this is; below the fold describes the copy — its manufacture and its condition. That is the line, and a build applies it without judgement: a fact that would let you tell this pressing from another pressing of the same title goes above the fold; a fact that only describes the object in your hands goes below. Label, catalogue number, country, year, format and matrix identify the pressing. Plant, weight and pressing colour describe the copy. Genres describe neither — they describe the work, and that is the third case the test needs, because the frame holds the record and only the region below holds the object. A genre is true of every pressing ever made of this title and of the music itself, so it cannot sort by a test about telling pressings apart; it belongs with title and artist, which are also facts about the work, and those are the frame's first two lines. The rule in its complete form, and scoped to what it was derived for — the pressing block, and nothing else: of the facts that would otherwise sit in that block, those true of the work or of the pressing stay above the fold, and those true only of the physical copy go below. Plant, weight and colour would differ on another copy of the same pressing; label, catalogue number, country, year, format, matrix and genres would not. 9.1 · Twelve columns, content-derived rows

The same repeat(12, 1fr) as §2.1, gap 0, full bleed, 34px padding inside every cell — and row heights derived from content instead of fixed. This replaces the 216px label rail, which was the wrong repair. The argument for it was that the twelve columns exist for a budget that does not apply below the fold — but that is an argument against the fixed bands, not against the columns. Bands are height and columns are alignment; dropping the budget only requires dropping the height. A rail aligned to nothing above it made the page a grid with a document stapled under it, and the frame's own record band is already what this region needed to be: twelve columns whose cells hold different things.

The rail survives as a span, not as a structure: the label occupies the first two columns of every section. Same reading — a label on the left, content to its right — and now its x is a column edge rather than an invented one, so it lines up with the identity cell above it. The colour bar sits under the label inside that span, which keeps §9.3 exactly as ruled.

Three content splits and no more, which is what stops each section inventing its own: the ten columns right of the label divide 10, or 5 + 5, or 6 + 4. The section picks by the shape of what it holds — 10 for one continuous thing (tags, thumbnails), 5 + 5 for two comparable things (pressing pairs, source/date, figure/action, series/rows), 6 + 4 for a body with an action beside it (snippet, journal). Four content shapes, three splits, no fourth pattern: label-value pairs and list rows both take 5 + 5 because a date-and-price row and a plant-and-value row are the same object, and a form takes the same 5 + 5 as the pairs it edits. A section needing a fourth split is a section whose content has not been identified yet.

Vertical rules return, because there are cells again: 1px oklch(0.72 0.004 80) on the right of every cell but the last, exactly as §2.1 draws them. That is the second thing the rail could not do — a rail has one edge and a grid has as many as it has cells, and the region now divides horizontally the way the frame does. Section boundaries stay as ruled: the full-bleed hairline of §3 running to the composition's edge while cell padding keeps content at 34px, which is the distinction §3 makes load-bearing and the one thing about the rail version that was right.

Height is derived at every level and nothing is reserved. A row is as tall as its tallest cell, a section as tall as its row, the region as tall as its sections; a form that grows pushes everything below it down. An empty section is not rendered at all — no diagonal, no label, no cell — and §6's one-diagonal rule still does not cross the fold, for the reason it did not before: above, absence must be drawn because the geometry is fixed; below, drawing it would be inventing content. A section with a control renders with the control alone.

9.2 · How an ornament gets a box when the box is not known until render

The frame's ornament rules assume a geometry this region does not have — every mark above the fold sits in a cell of stated height, and below it a cell is 40px on one record and 300px on another. The answer is that ornament here is sized by width and gated by height, because width is the one dimension the region inherited as fixed. Twelve columns at 120px are known before any content renders; heights are not. So an in-cell solid is sized at 0.62 × its section's height — 0.627 of the cell it sits in, the two differing because the section carries the 1px border-top — its width follows the archetype rather than a fixed ratio, and it is placed against the cell's bottom-right corner bleeding past it. This sentence read “0.62 × its cell's height” and “width follows from that height by the projection (h / 1.06)” until now; both are corrected later in this section, which left the first sizing rule a reader meets stating the superseded base and the withdrawn constant. On the four carrying cells that is 79, 72, 76 and 94 tall — height is the primary term and width is derived from it, which is the opposite of the withdrawn rule and the reason the ruled value survives a round trip: the first implementation of this computed height, derived width, then re-derived height from that width, inflating every solid by a pixel and drawing 0.63 where the rule says 0.62. Height rather than width, and that is §10.3's ruling applied here rather than described elsewhere: the earlier rule said half a column, which is a width, and in cells eleven times wider than tall the dimension carrying presence is height — the granularity finding below, arriving one level out. A prior correction in the same term is worth keeping because it is the reason the rule is stated relatively at all: at a fixed 120px a solid is 127px tall, and once the gate and the control clearance are both applied there is exactly one cell in the region it can legally occupy. A rule permitting one position is not a rule about where ornament goes.

Two shapes recorded, both from this subsection's four rounds. The first is new and is not the derived-from-a-set defect §9.4 carries: a rule whose parameters make it vacuous, where every attempt to fix the rule is working on the wrong term. The tell is a rule that keeps needing loosening and keeps not converging — four rounds amounting to relaxing a gate and a clearance that were both correct, against a size that was not. Check the parameters before the predicate when a rule will not settle.

The second explains the other two restatements and is the more transferable: a rule stated at a coarser granularity than its rationale excludes cases the rationale permits. The gate named the solid's total box when its reason is about how much of the solid a reader sees, and the bottom bleed is deliberate — so no one-column solid could pass. The clearance named cell membership when its reason is competition with a hit target, and a cell can be 600px wide and hold a 44px button — so it barred a solid 200px clear of it. The check is whether the thing the rule names is the thing the reason is about: distance rather than membership, visible height rather than total box, and the unit a parameter is set in rather than a unit it merely resembles. No ornament is ever measured against a height it cannot know.

The gate is a ratio, measured on the solid's visible height: a solid renders only where the part of it inside the cell is at most two-thirds of the cell's height, and suppresses entirely above that. Visible, not total, because the bottom bleed is deliberate — an earlier version of this sentence gated the cell at 1.5 × the solid's whole box, which no one-column solid can ever satisfy (127px box needs a 190px cell; the tallest non-control cell in the region is 175px), so the rule as first written permitted zero positions while the drawing showed two. The ratio is also the right thing to measure: the gate exists so ornament does not dominate the cell it decorates, which is a proportion rather than a clearance. Under a height-relative size the gate binds at one value: the solid is 0.62 × its section's height, which is 0.627 of the cell it sits in, against a ceiling of two-thirds. Section, not cell, and the two differ: the section carries the 1px border-top and its cells resolve sub-pixel, so a cell is about 1.2px shorter than the section it belongs to — 126.4 against 127. The rule names the section because that is the height a build has before the cells lay out, and the gate's ceiling is checked against the cell, which is the box the solid is clipped by.

One mechanism note, recorded here rather than at the call site, because the next reader of "0.62 of the section's height" will reach for the same thing: do not use container-type: size. It expresses the rule with no measurement at all, which is why it is the obvious choice, and it collapses the layout — size containment removes a cell's contents from its own height calculation, so a 140px section renders at 69 and the solid at 0 × 0. A percentage height with aspect-ratio for the width does the same job without the circularity and keeps the component server-side.

The archetype varies and the source is the cell, not the record. All four solids being the same cube is a defect — the frame's generator has six archetypes and §9 was using none — but the fix has to survive the size rule, which describes a cube and nothing else. So height stays the governed term at 0.62 of the section, and width follows the archetype rather than a fixed 1.06: a beam at 0.8 × 0.8 × 7.6 draws wide and shallow, a plate at 2.8 × 2.8 × 0.28 draws wide and flat, an upright panel draws narrow. Height carries presence, so fixing height is what preserves the rule; h / 1.06 was never the rule, it was the cube's instance of it. It was not even that by the time it was written: the redrawn cube is 66 × 76, a ratio of 0.87. So the constant was not a rule over-generalised from one case — it was a rule over-generalised from a case that had since moved, which is worse, because a reader checking it against the drawing would have found it wrong for the member it names.

The gate takes no archetype and that is deliberate. It asks what fraction of the cell's height the solid occupies, and height is governed at 0.62 for every archetype — so the answer cannot depend on which one is drawn. Threading an archetype through it would imply it could, which is a worse defect than the redundancy it removes. A fifth archetype needs a width relationship and nothing else.

Which archetype a cell gets is fixed per section and written down: Pressing detail the beam, Snippet the plate, Market the cube, Price history the panel — drawn at 115 × 79, 113 × 72, 66 × 76 and 48 × 94, one height rule and four silhouettes. The first version of this rule derived the archetype from the cell's proportion — beam for a wide cell, cube for a square one — which is a better rule and is vacuous here: the four carrying cells measure 4.75, 6.27, 4.92 and 4.00 : 1, so every one of them is "wide" and the rule yields one archetype for all four, plus no verdict at all at exactly 4.00. A rule that derives variety from an axis the real set does not vary along produces none — the shape §9.2 already records twice, arriving a third time in the round that cites it. The assignment is therefore a decision rather than a derivation, which is also the honest description of what it is.

Not per record, and that is the same ruling §9.4 made for the bars. A hashed or per-record archetype makes the region's ornament encode which record you are on — and the page already has a thing that does that, deliberately and at scale: the construction in the frame. The frame's ornament is the record's; the region's is the page's. The region is an index, and an index whose decoration changes per entry is asking to be read as data. Two marks encoding identity in two vocabularies would also undo the one-vocabulary ruling by the back door. That is the gate doing the work it was written for rather than passing trivially — at the withdrawn fixed size the four cells measured 0.40, 0.39, 0.43 and 0.32, so no placement could ever have failed it. 0.62 rather than something nearer the ceiling is a rounding margin, not a maximum: 0.65 passes a two-thirds gate too, but cell heights are sub-pixel and the solid's is an integer, so a value that close crosses the ceiling on some cells and not others — and a gate that fails by rounding is the coincidence-for-margin defect the title band and the year field each produced once. The drawn ratios are 0.627 on three cells and 0.623 on the fourth. One test, one number, applied per cell at render — not a whitelist of sections with a content floor, which was the other candidate and is the enumeration defect §9.4 records: a section's height varies by record, so a rule naming sections decides on the schema what only the record can answer. The gate is also why suppression is safe here, and §5.4 agrees rather than forbidding it: “decoration does not decorate an absence” requires the edge fields to suppress when their cell is empty, on the identical ground — ornament carries no data — so a record whose Acquisition cell is one line short simply has no solid there. What forbids suppression is §6, and only for a fact: an absent fact must be visible rather than silent, which is why a diagonal fires where a solid does not. An earlier version of this sentence had §5.4 forbidding it, which is the reverse of its source and would have told a build the file’s ornament-suppression rule was a prohibition.

Flat edge fields attach to the region, not to a cell, and bleed off the composition's bottom edge. That is the one place below the fold where a height is known — the region's own end — and it is also what §5.1's frame rule requires: a mark touching a page edge is a flat plane, a mark not touching it is an isometric solid. So the division of labour is exactly the frame's, re-derived from what each kind can be measured against: solids go in cells because a column width is enough to size them, and planes go on the region's edges because a cell's edge is not the composition's.

Everything is tint step and nothing is base. The three faces of a solid are three lightnesses of the one tint value, not three hues — which is §5.3's argument that a form reads as an object because its tones are lightness steps. That keeps §9.4's base-mark count at four and untouched: ornament is ground, the bars are the record's colour arriving, and a reader can still tell which is which because one kind sits in the label column and the other never does.

One per section, and no solid may intersect a control's box or come within half a column of it — the same unit the solid is sized in. The constraint is proximity, not cell membership — an earlier version of this sentence said "never in the same cell as a control", which is coarser than its own reason: a cell can be 600px wide and hold a 44px button, so cell membership bars a solid 200px clear of the thing it is meant to protect. The reason is that a solid behind or beside a file picker or a submit is decoration competing with a hit target, and §9.3's controls are the one thing in this region a reader must find first — which makes the test a distance, measured against the control's box. A ruled input fails it at any distance, because the field spans its cell's full inner width — so Acquisition carries no solid in either cell, which is where the first version of this drawing put one, painting over 120px of the Date field's underline. The uploader and the textarea fail the same way by filling their cells, so Images and Journal carry none, and Tags is excluded by its + add chip. The four that remain are the cells holding only type: Pressing detail's pairs, Market's figure, Snippet's prose, Price history's series. Drawn here: half-column solids in Pressing detail, Market, Snippet and Price history, and two quarter-circles on the region's bottom edge. Ornament is a layer, and the layer is structural rather than a set of z-indexes on the elements that happened to collide. Every cell carries position: relative; isolation: isolate; overflow: hidden — relative to be the containing block, isolate to be a stacking context so a negative index cannot escape to an ancestor, hidden to clip what bleeds — and every ornament sits at z-index: -1 inside it — behind all of the cell's content and in front of the cell's own background, with nothing lifted and nothing enumerated. Controls, ruled fields, chips, uploaders, textareas and type runs are above ornament because they are in flow, not because each was named: the first implementation of this lifted elements by matching their heights and missed the textarea, the uploader and the tag chips — the enumeration defect, inside the paragraph that disavows it. The three declarations are stated here rather than left to the build because a paragraph forbidding per-element lifts has to say what replaces them, and this defect has now occurred twice: three patches in the frame, eleven lifts here.

One note on how the gate was tested, because the distinction survives as prose while the code does something else. A test named for measuring the solid's visible part passed against a gate written on its total height: the two differ only where the cell is shorter than the solid, and there both reject anyway, so no test of the gate's boolean can distinguish visible from total. The distinction is real and lives in the ratio, which is where it is asserted. A test that exercises a rule's output cannot check a premise the output is insensitive to — assert the intermediate value, not the verdict. The frame needed the same fix and it failed there first as three patches, which is why it is stated as a layer here: a per-collision z-index is correct until the next placement moves, and a layer is correct for placements nobody has drawn yet.

9.3 · Controls, which 8a had never drawn

One control vocabulary: 44px tall, 1px ink box, no fill, no radius, 11px mono uppercase ink label — and box-sizing: border-box, so the authored height is the rendered one. The box is §3's hairline weight at ink rather than grey, which is the smallest possible difference that says pressable on a page built from hairlines; a filled button would be the only solid non-derived mass on the page and would read as a colour mark. 44px satisfies the hit-target floor and is also 4× the label's 11px, so the control's height is on the type scale rather than beside it.

Inputs are ruled fields, not boxes: a 1px ink underline under a 16px line, 34px tall — sans where the field takes prose, mono where it takes data, which is §4.1's split applying to entry exactly as it applies to display, so Source is sans and Date is mono. Text entry belongs to the type system, and a box around a line of type is a second frame around something the cell already framed.

16px, not 13, and the conflict is with §4 rather than with A67. A67 is a correctness rule with a named browser failure — Safari zooms a focused input under 16px — and a type-system claim does not outrank a browser that moves the page. But the honest statement of the cost is not "3px larger": it is that 16 is not on the scale at all, which §4.1 states as nothing between 40 and 15. So this is a scale amendment and is written as one: the scale admits 16 for the typed line of an input, and for nothing else. Not for labels, not for prose, not for a heading, not for the field's label above it, and not for read-only data that happens to sit in a field-shaped box. The scope is exactly "text the user can put a cursor in", which is the scope of the browser behaviour that forces it — a size introduced to satisfy a rule must be bounded by that rule's own reach, or the next value with a good reason arrives at 14 and the scale is a suggestion. Geometry is unaffected and the drawing carries it: at 16px/1.9 the line is 30.4px inside a 34px border-box field with a 1px bottom border. The three typed lines above — Source, Date, the journal placeholder — are the whole scope of the 16 in this composition, and they are drawn at it rather than described at it, because this file is the build reference and a build reads the drawing.

Three exceptions, each for a reason the vocabulary cannot cover. The journal textarea takes a full 1px grey box, because it is a region of text rather than a line and a region with only a baseline has no shape at all. The uploader is a dashed 1px grey square at the thumbnail's exact size — same footprint as what it will become, which is §6's sleeve-placeholder rule applied to a control. Tag chips are 30px rather than 44px, because a chip is a value with an affordance attached and not a control; the + add chip is the same box at 1px dashed muted.

Destructive actions are identical to constructive ones. §5.5 excludes anything constant across records from taking colour, and Delete is on every record — so it cannot be red here without red becoming a second non-derived hue on a page keyed to one. The confirmation carries the weight instead, which is where it belongs: a button cannot be dangerous, only an action can.

A form's submit is not the trigger that opened it, and the two never share a label. The frame's journal cell carries Add entry as the trigger (§8.1); this region's Journal section carries Save entry, which is the submit. Drawn with the trigger's label it would be the same seam §8.1 removed — one control announced in two places 900px apart — and a reader who has already pressed Add entry is not adding anything by pressing it again.

9.4 · Where the colour goes, and the floor that does not apply

One base-step mark per qualifying section, in the label span under the label: a 44 × 10 bar. The full fill is admitted, once per region, and it is tint — which means it is not a mark and does not displace one. Base was the tempting answer and it is wrong twice: it would put a second mass at the record's colour, and §5.2's lightness derivation depends on the year field being the only base mark carrying type. The objection to tint — that ground is too weak to anchor — mistakes how the two anchor: a mark anchors by contrast, ground anchors by area, and a tint plane at cell scale has forty times a bar's area. That is why it can be the region's floor at a step the bars would be invisible at. It fills the last section's widest cell, one per region rather than one per section, and the bar in that section stays — a mark and its ground are different objects, so no count is affected. Last, because §10.3 orders the region by density and a page thinning downward needs something to stop on. It sits there because the label span is the constant — a mark at the label column's x sits on the one line the reader has already learned, so it reads as the colour arriving again rather than as a new mark, which is what §5.5's distribution rule asks of the frame and what no single large mark can do.

One test applied to all eight: a section carries the mark when it holds a fact that appears nowhere above the fold. Marked — Pressing detail (plant, weight and colour are drawn in no frame cell), Images (the frame shows a count, never the images), Price history (the frame shows one figure, never the series), Journal (the frame shows no entry at all), Snippet (§10b's generated text is a column of its own and no frame cell has ever drawn it). Unmarked — Acquisition and Market, because the frame draws provenance’s three facts and the median and those sections only edit and refresh them; Tags, because a tag is a control and not a fact (§8.1). Five of eight, and the test decides each section without reference to any other.

Two of those grounds were false statements about the build and they moved the verdict in both directions. Snippet was unmarked because "its text is exactly what the frame's journal cell draws" — the cell drew record.notes, the owner's own text, and the snippet is record.snippet, a separate generated column the frame has never shown. Snippet was always marked under this predicate; the file said otherwise for four rounds. And Journal was marked because "the frame's journal cell shows the snippet, not an entry" — the cell drew an entry, above the note, so the verdict was right and the reason named neither of the two facts the cell actually held.

The shape, and it is not one of the seven §9.2 records: a correct predicate correctly applied to a false fact about the build. Nothing in the chain fails — the rule is sound, the application is sound, the input is wrong — so it survived four rounds of review of the rule it justifies. A predicate gets checked against its cases and the cases against each other, while the sentence asserting what the build draws sits outside both loops. The check is mechanical and belongs in review: for every ground of the form "because the frame shows X", find X in the frame. And what exposed it was a change rather than a review — removing the journal cell forced the question of what that cell had shown, which four readings of the predicate never asked.

Five is what the rule yields, not a target. Four was an observation of the output when the output was wrong, and this paragraph states no minimum and no maximum: a predicate that decides each section on its own cannot also promise a count.

The predicate is evaluated against the schema, not against the record — a section carries the mark when the kind of fact it holds appears nowhere above the fold, whether or not this record has one. So Images, Journal and Price history are marked on all seventeen, including the sixteen where Images and Journal are control-only. The decisive argument is that the alternative makes the mark encode data. A bar that appears when a record has images and vanishes when it does not is an indicator of that fact, and nothing on this page encodes anything — §4 refuses a third 72 for that reason, §5.3 refuses to drop the coverless record's marks for it, and the construction fixes its form count for it. A mark whose presence is information is a different kind of object from a mark that says which record you are looking at.

The measurement makes this load-bearing rather than a detail: on the modal record only four sections render and all four are control-only, so on sixteen of seventeen records the marked set is Images and Journal alone. Evaluating per-record would give the modal page zero marks, which retires the repetition the floor exemption rests on and leaves the region with no derived colour at all on almost the whole collection. Two marks at a constant x is the real repetition this region ships — the drawing's four is the richest record only, and the argument below is made at two.

A mark beside a control-only section is not a mark beside nothing. A section renders when it has content or a control (§9.1), and a control-only Images section is still where this record's images go — the bar says the record's colour reaches that place, not that something is in it. This is §5.3's ruling one region out: there the coverless record keeps all its marks because a missing image may not change the composition's structure, and here an imageless record keeps its label-span mark for the same reason.

A mark on every section would be texture, which §5.5's reasoning retires. And the repetition case is positional rather than numerical — an earlier version of this passage put it as a count, four repetitions being the minimum that reads. That was smuggled in from thinking of the bars as standalone marks, and it would mean the region falls below its own floor on the sixteen records that ship two, which is the opposite of what the exemption exists for. The label column is what repeats; the bars only have to sit in it. Two marks far apart at an x the reader has met in eight labels are the same event as four, because the thing establishing the position is the column and not the marks. No minimum count is specified, and none should be implemented.

§5.5's 0.5%-of-page floor does not govern below the fold, and the reason is the floor's own argument. It exists because a mark too small to find in a fixed composition is not arriving at all — findability inside one screenful. Below the fold the marks repeat in a column, and position does the work area did above. A 44 × 10 bar is 0.02% of the region and findable because it sits in the label column — the one x this region trains the reader to read down, and the same x whether a record ships two bars or four. Measuring it against the frame's floor would be the same defect this file has recorded seven times — applying a rule across an axis it was never derived over.

Recorded as its own shape, distinct from the six this file already carries: those were operations defined over an axis merely correlated with the signal; this is a rule derived from a set, which will always fit that set and may fit nothing else. The tell is that it cannot be applied to a member you have not already decided — the test for any rule stated over an enumeration is to hand it a section the drawing does not contain. Three versions of the mark rule failed that test before the drawing changed instead of the rule.

10.1 · Two rhythms, and what the whole view shows

The two rhythms are real and the measurement names them, read off this view: the frame's cells run 1.1 : 1 height to width — 1.14, 1.52, 0.91 across the upper band and 0.83, 1.25, 1.25, 1.25, 0.83 across the record band, with three of the eight wider than tall — against the region's sections at 11 : 1 width to height, ranging 4.3 to 15.9. An earlier version of this sentence said 1.8 : 1 and 1 : 3.5, both estimated rather than measured and the second inverted; the corrected figures make the break larger than the wrong ones claimed, which is the argument rather than a qualification of it. Same twelve columns, boxes above and rows below, and the cause is the ruling — bands are height, columns are alignment, so dropping the budget dropped the height only.

Seen whole, though, the break is not at the fold. It is at the point the content thins, which is four sections down on the richest record and immediately on the modal one. The frame reads as boxes because it holds eleven facts in 847px; the region reads as rows because most of its sections hold one line. A rhythm built for thin content is what thin content should look like, and forcing a minimum row height would reintroduce the void the frame spent six rounds removing — in a region with no budget to justify it.

So it stands, with the alignment doing the work, and one thing added rather than changed: the region's first section is the one that has to bridge. 10.3 · Which, and the rule that replaces half a column

A is the answer, and the reason is the granularity finding arriving one level out: half a column is a width, and in a band eleven times wider than tall the dimension that carries presence is height. The rule was stated in the unit the frame's roughly-square cells made indifferent, and the region's cells are not indifferent to it. So an in-cell solid is sized at 0.62 × its section's height — 0.627 of the cell — with width following the archetype (this read “0.62 × its cell's height, width following from that height by the projection” until now, in different words from §9.2's, which is why correcting §9.2 left it standing) — 79, 72, 76 and 94 tall on the four cells that carry one, against 64 for all four before. The gate is unchanged and is now doing real work rather than passing trivially: 0.627 visible against a two-thirds ceiling is the binding constraint it was written to be.

B fails on the thing it is trying to fix. Three marks at the drawn size read as a row of specks rather than as one mark — it converts a presence problem into a pattern, and a repeating motif at the bottom of every wide section is a texture, which §5.1 excludes for the same reason the constant provenance diagonal was removed: a mark that appears at a fixed interval stops being a mark. The one-per-section cap holds.

C is the strongest of the three to look at and it breaks the rule that earned its place by looking. A flat field crossing the band is a plane arriving from outside the frame, and these fields arrive from inside it — the section has a left and right edge that are the composition's edges only at the ends. Flat means crossing the page edge; a flat field stopping mid-page is the second vocabulary the matrix solid settled against. It stays available for the region's bottom edge, where it already is, because that edge is the composition's.

What changes in §9.2: the size term only. 0.62 × section height, 0.627 of the cell, replaces half a column; the gate, the half-column clearance from controls, the one-per-section cap, the tint-step ruling and the four carrying sections are all unchanged. Vocabulary is unchanged too — same projection, same three lightnesses, same corner, same bleed — which is what keeps the region's marks reading as the frame's at a size the frame never needed.
