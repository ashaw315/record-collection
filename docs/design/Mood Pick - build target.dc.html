P.1 · Where the feature is ruled, and where it is presented

The feature is ruled here, once, and in no other target. What the control does, the lean, that it is not a filter, surprise me, and the states are the same whatever presents the answer, so they have one home. Ruled across the wall and the table and grid, each target would hold half, and whatever one ruled the other would restate, and a restatement drifts. The modal is the precedent: it has its own target, and it points at the wall for what it shares rather than ruling it again. The scope’s retirement asks for the same thing, a control in one place, and here that is one target to close.

There are two presentations, and on the shelf the answer is the pull. A record leaving its seat is the answer to being asked. A marked row is a result, and that is the table’s job. The pull is §W’s, and this target points at it and does not restate its figures. Where the shelf is not the view, the answer is presented in §T’s vocabulary, by §P.4.

The split is the reader’s view, and §W.24’s condition decides where the shelf can be one. The pull answers a reader on the shelf. Every other reader is answered in place, on the table or the grid they are on, including a reader where §W.24 offers the shelf and they have not chosen it. An ask does not move them to another view or discard their sort, scroll or filters. That is Adam’s answer: a reader who wants the pull asks from the shelf. So the second presentation reaches more than a phone: a tablet, a narrow desktop window, and a reader on the table or the grid at any width. The condition belongs to §W.24, so when that section moves, this split moves with it and nothing here is rewritten.

This target owns what belongs to the feature, and points for the rest. It owns the control, its states, and what a pick shows when it arrives. The pull’s motion, timing and landing stay §W’s, and the table and grid’s type, colour and spacing stay §T’s. Where a section pointed at does not reach the pick’s case, the difference is ruled here, in place. The out-of-frame seat is not such a case: Code measured §W.22’s pan reaching every out-of-frame seat it tried, landing as an in-frame pull does.

P.2 · What an ask does

The moods toggle, and a separate ASK asks. Pressing a mood selects or clears it and asks nothing. Pressing ASK asks, and pressing it again asks again. A mood that asked when pressed would make choosing two moods impossible, and a second press on SLEEPY would mean either ask again or clear it. ASK is inert while no mood is selected. This is Adam’s.

The ask leans; it does not filter. Each record in the pool is scored by how many of the selected moods it carries, and the pick is drawn at random from the highest score. A record carrying none of them is never drawn. So an ask has an answer whenever any record in the pool carries any selected mood, at two moods or at eight. Adding a mood can still shrink the highest score’s tier, where some records carry both, so the lean is not a widening either. It only never empties. Nothing on the control may imply narrowing: no count beside a mood, no ALL or AND, no figure that changes as moods are added.

The same record is not served twice in a row while another remains. The page holds one last-served record, set by every answer, ASK’s and SURPRISE ME’s alike. The pick excludes it, unless excluding it would leave nothing, when it draws from the whole tier and may serve it again. A record the reader pulled by its spine counts as served too, since the annoyance Adam names is the same record twice in a row, and pulling one, asking, and getting it back is that. The rule is Adam’s; the spine clause is mine, from his reason.

The pool is what the screen shows. Under a filter or a search, an ask draws from the matches. The answer arrives on the screen the reader is on, and a record that screen has hidden would be pulled from a seat the filter emptied, or shown beside a grid that left it out. The moods themselves change nothing on the screen: no seat empties, no row or cover hides, and the count does not move.

It is not a filter in any other respect. The selected moods and the last-served record are held by the page only. Nothing goes to the address, a stored preference or the Sort control, and a navigation clears them. This is Adam’s.

SURPRISE ME draws from the whole pool, tagged or not, ignoring the moods and leaving them selected, under the same last-served rule.

No entry: nothing was ruled here before. What is new is the ask, the lean, the last-served rule and the pool.

P.3 · A second ask on the shelf

On the shelf the first answer is §W’s pull, from the seat the pick names. Where that seat is out of frame, §W.22’s pan reaches it. At 22 records that happens only after the reader has scrolled; at 200 it happens at rest.

With a record out, an ask puts it back and then pulls the next, both by §W unchanged. That holds whether the record came out by an ask or by its spine. §W.8 already says why this is a put back and a pull and not a slide, in the sentence quoted below, and §W.9 places the arrows with the record for the same reason: a pick names any seat, so its answer goes out of the collection and into it, not along it. Withdrawn within §P.3: the slide’s reason cited to §W.9. The sentence is §W.8’s, in the paragraph that keeps the arrows, under that section’s accessibility heading; §W.9 rules only where the arrows sit. The citation was carried in the drop that brought this target, so the entry is filed; over-filing costs nothing. So the arrows stay the neighbour’s slide, and a spine pressed while a record is out stays refused, as built. The pick needs neither changed, and borrowing the arrows’ slide for a distant seat would set the pick against the reason the slide was given.

No entry: nothing was ruled here before. No target ruled what happens on a second ask. What is new is the re-roll as a put back and then a pull, the ask ignored while a record moves, and the record already out.

A re-roll costs the put back and the pull together, and the order between them is provisional. The pull could begin before the put back ends. Whether it should, and by how much, is motion, which §W.25 says a still cannot judge. Until Adam has watched the probe in this section’s step, they run in sequence, and that step’s report edits this paragraph. The probe judges the repeated press, not a single one. The feature’s loop is pressing until something appeals, a re-roll costs two motions, and the paragraph below ignores an ask while either runs, so the loop can feel sticky in a way neither motion shows alone.

An ask while a record is moving is ignored, as §M.5 ignores a press that would start another motion. A mood pressed meanwhile is honoured, because it moves nothing. Whether an ignored ask is right in the repeated press is part of what the probe above settles, so this paragraph is provisional with it.

Where the pick names the record already out, nothing moves. By §P.2’s rule that happens only where the tier holds that one record. The slot beneath the control says so, so a record that stays still reads as an answer and not as a press that failed.

The unexplained put back. Code recorded, once in three runs, a put back that left the wall 455px off, in a synthetic 200-record case. A re-roll puts back on every press, so a rare fault on the spine’s path sits on this feature’s main loop. It is §W’s to explain, and it is recorded in the handoff’s open questions. This section’s step tries to reproduce it and reports.

P.4 · The answer on the table and the grid

On the table and the grid the answer is one cover, beneath the control. It is §T.5’s cell: the cover, its title and artist beneath it, and the whole cell one link to its record. §W.24 calls the grid the looking view, and a record handed to the reader is a thing to look at. A row marked in the table would be a result, which is what the table already gives.

Nothing else on the page changes. The view, the sort, the filters and the scroll stay as they were, and nothing scrolls to the record in the list. The answer is content the reader asked for, so by §T.6 it may push what follows it, and the first answer does. A later answer replaces it in the same place. Whether a different caption can change the cell’s height, and so move what follows again, is measured by the step, not predicted here.

P.5 · The control

The control is the eight moods, then ASK and SURPRISE ME, then the reserved slot, in that order in every host. The moods are one list in code, so the control draws whatever that list holds, in its order, and a renamed mood needs no ruling here.

On the shelf it sits in the rail, beneath the view names and above ADD RECORD, which stays at the foot. It is kept apart from SEARCH, GENRE and SORT because it is not a filter. On the table and the grid it sits beneath the last filter line, after a hairline: in the sidebar above the sidebar fork, and in the band below it. There it stands with the filters only because that is where those screens keep their controls, and the hairline is what separates it.

A mood is set in the label type, in the label colour when clear and in ink when selected, with no bar, underline or box. The bar and the underline are what a current view and an active filter wear, by §W.13 and §W.32, and a mood wearing either would read as a filter. ASK and SURPRISE ME are §9.3 controls. The hit areas follow the handoff’s rules for the floor and for hit areas in a set, which this section points at and does not restate.

Whether the control fits the rail is measured, not predicted. The step measures it against the rail’s width and the rail’s free height at the narrowest window that offers the shelf, and stops and reports if it does not fit.

No entry: nothing was ruled here before. The rail, the sidebar and the band gain a block, and no sentence that orders them is changed by it.

P.6 · The states

A mood is inert when no tagged record carries it. It stays drawn at its size in the label colour, cannot be selected, and is named as unavailable to assistive technology. Inertness is counted over the whole collection, not over what a filter leaves, so a filter never changes the control.

Whether an inert mood is empty or waiting is a fact about the collection, not about the mood. A record not yet tagged could carry any mood, so while any record is untagged no mood can honestly be called empty. So the difference is drawn once, for the collection. While any record is untagged, a line beneath the moods gives how many are not yet tagged, and every inert mood reads under it as possibly waiting. With every record tagged the line is absent, and an inert mood is empty. Some tagged and some not is the normal state between adding a record and running the script. The line is drawn when the page loads, never in answer to a press, so it moves nothing a reader is about to press.

An untagged record is reached only by SURPRISE ME, which ignores tags, so no record is out of reach while the script has not run.

With nothing tagged, the control is SURPRISE ME alone, with no moods, no ASK and no line. Eight words that cannot be pressed would be a control drawn to be refused. It is also Phase 0’s form, so Phase 0 builds it first, and the moods join when the first tags land.

An ask the pool cannot answer draws nothing. Where the selected moods have records but none among what the screen shows, there is no pull and no cell, and the slot says nothing showing carries these moods.

The slot’s messages are written to fit it. The slot is §T.6’s reserved slot, and its rule that a message is rewritten rather than cut reaches the rail, where one line is narrow. Proposed: “The only one.”, “None showing.”, and for the line, “4 not tagged yet.” The step checks each at the rail’s width and at 320 and 1280.

Reduced motion is §W.8’s. Under it the wall moves nothing on its own, so a re-roll’s put back and pull both cut, and the next record is simply out. On the table and the grid an answer never moves. Nothing is ruled here.

No entry: nothing was ruled here before. What is new is the inert mood, the untagged line, the surprise-only form and the unanswerable ask.
