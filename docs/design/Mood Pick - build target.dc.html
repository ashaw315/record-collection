P.2 · What an ask does

The moods toggle, and a separate ASK asks. Pressing a mood selects or clears it and asks nothing. Pressing ASK asks, and pressing it again asks again. A mood that asked when pressed would make choosing two moods impossible, and a second press on SLEEPY would mean either ask again or clear it. ASK is inert while no mood is selected. This is Adam’s.

The ask leans; it does not filter. Each record in the pool is scored by how many of the selected moods it carries, and the pick is drawn at random from the highest score. A record carrying none of them is never drawn. So an ask has an answer whenever any record in the pool carries any selected mood, at two moods or at eight. Adding a mood can still shrink the highest score’s tier, where some records carry both, so the lean is not a widening either. It only never empties. Nothing on the control may imply narrowing: no count beside a mood, no ALL or AND, no figure that changes as moods are added.

The same record is not served twice in a row while another remains. The page holds one last-served record, set by every answer, ASK’s and SURPRISE ME’s alike. The pick excludes it, unless excluding it would leave nothing, when it draws from the whole tier and may serve it again. A record the reader pulled by its spine counts as served too, since the annoyance Adam names is the same record twice in a row, and pulling one, asking, and getting it back is that. A record brought out by §W.9’s arrows counts as served for the same reason: arrowing to it and then asking is the same record twice in a row. The rule is Adam’s; the spine clause is mine, from his reason.

Withdrawn within §P.2: the pool as what the screen shows. A paged table shows one page, and the pool is every match on every page. It was carried, so the entry is owed. The pool is every record the filters and the search leave, on every page. Under a filter or a search, an ask draws from the matches. The answer arrives on the screen the reader is on, and a record that screen has hidden would be pulled from a seat the filter emptied, or shown beside a grid that left it out. Paging is not filtering. A filter or a search is the reader saying which records they mean, and the pick honours it. A page is the table’s way of fitting those records to the window, a choice of the screen’s length and not of the reader’s, so it constrains nothing. A page-two record hides no seat, since the shelf is unpaged and seats every match, and it needs no row, since the table’s answer is §P.4’s cell beneath the control, not a row on the page. Holding the pool to the page would make an ask depend on page size and sort, and change its pool on every page turned. Code built it this way, and this is the reading ruled. The moods themselves change nothing on the screen: no seat empties, no row or cover hides, and the count does not move.

It is not a filter in any other respect. The selected moods and the last-served record are held by the page only. Nothing goes to the address, a stored preference or the Sort control, and a navigation clears them. This is Adam’s. A zoom between the near and the far view is not a navigation for this rule, though §W.29 writes it to the address. What §W.29 writes there is the view, not the pick, so nothing of the pick goes to the address. §P.1 keeps the view apart from the pick, and clearing on a zoom would let a reader zoom, ask and get back the record just served, which is what the last-served rule prevents. So the selected moods and the last-served record survive a zoom. An answer already drawn ends when its host changes, and the new host starts with none. A zoom changes the host, and so does a resize across a fork that moves the control, such as the far view narrowing past 1273 from the column into the band, or the table crossing the sidebar fork. The answer’s place no longer exists, so keeping the cell where the old host put it would be keeping a place the screen no longer has. A resize within one host keeps the answer. The cost: a reader who resizes across a fork loses the answer, and since it stays last-served, asking again will not hand it back while another remains. A cell on the far view is gone when the reader zooms in, and the near view shows no answer until the next ask. Withdrawn within §P.2: a record the pick pulled not held out when the reader zooms out, and the wall’s part left to an open question. The sentence meant the record, not the pick’s claim on it, and Code measured the wall holding the record out. §W.12 now rules it. It was carried, so the entry is owed. What ends with the host is the answer, the pick’s claim on the record, and not the record’s place. A record the pick pulled stops being an answer when the reader zooms out or resizes across a fork. It stays out, held by the wall by §W.12, as a record pulled by its spine or by the arrows does. The far view does not draw it and draws no cell for it, and it is out again when the reader zooms back in. P.6 · The states

A mood is inert when no tagged record carries it. It stays drawn at its size in the label colour, cannot be selected, and is named as unavailable to assistive technology. An inert mood stays a Tab stop, and so does ASK while it is inert. A walk whose stops came and went as Adam tagged records would move every later stop, and a keyboard reader learns the rail by its order. So the rail’s stops are fixed by the control’s list and not by the tags. ASK while inert is named as unavailable in the same way, with the reason as its description: choose a mood first. A reader who lands on it then hears why it will not act, as a sighted reader sees from no mood being in ink. Inertness is counted over the whole collection, not over what a filter leaves, so a filter never changes the control.

Whether an inert mood is empty or waiting is a fact about the collection, not about the mood. A record not yet tagged could carry any mood, so while any record is untagged no mood can honestly be called empty. So the difference is drawn once, for the collection. While any record is untagged, a line beneath the moods gives how many are not yet tagged, and every inert mood reads under it as possibly waiting. With every record tagged the line is absent, and an inert mood is empty. Some tagged and some not is the normal state between adding a record and running the script. The line is drawn when the page loads, never in answer to a press, so it moves nothing a reader is about to press.

An untagged record is reached only by SURPRISE ME, which ignores tags, so no record is out of reach while the script has not run.

With nothing tagged, the control is SURPRISE ME alone, with no moods, no ASK and no line. Eight words that cannot be pressed would be a control drawn to be refused. The form stands for a collection with no tags. Withdrawn within §P.6: Phase 0 building the control in its nothing-tagged form, with the moods joining when the first tags land.
